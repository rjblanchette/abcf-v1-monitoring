## Section 3 — Field convergence assessment

For each concept cluster with source diversity ≥ 2:

### Structural attribution limit / accountability incompleteness (P1/P5)
- **Convergence type:** Independent parallel, with a sequential-elaboration sub-thread (Tibebu → Solozobov series → DEMM-Bench).
- **Convergence depth:** Structural (same architectural claim: attribution/evidence gaps are structural, not fixable by more instrumentation) across most sources; the EU legal instruments are Formal in a different sense — they encode the claim into binding procedural/liability rules rather than proving it.
- **RJB priority position:** P1 (March 2026) and P5 (2026) both predate the Runs 11–14 additions (Solozobov series mid-2026; Reg. 2026/1755, July 2026; revised PLD analyses, March–June 2026 with December 2026 transposition). Priority dates remain clearly Zenodo-timestamped and earlier than every converging source identified.
- **Remaining gap:** The ontological rank-deficiency characterization (SLT/SI) and the liability-sink construction as a *named structural object* (not merely an outcome) remain unreached by any source, including the legal instruments, which reallocate consequences without characterizing the underlying indeterminacy formally.

### Execution-gate / rooted-authorization operationalization (P1/P2/P3)
- **Convergence type:** Independent parallel across at least four distinct starting points (formal transition systems — Fernandez; identity-governance infrastructure — Tallam; standards/protocol engineering — WIMSE/klrc-aiagent-auth; embodied-agent robotics — Governable Individuals), with sequential elaboration within the WIMSE thread itself (cross-org delegation → layered mapping → adoption call → source-level normative text).
- **Convergence depth:** Structural, and in the WIMSE §10.7 and Governable Individuals cases, arguably approaching **Formal** — both specify a precise gating condition (verifiable grant; signed lifecycle transition) rather than a general principle.
- **RJB priority position:** P1 (March 2026), P2 (March–April 2026), P3 (April 2026) all predate every item in this cluster; the most recent convergence (Governable Individuals v1, July 2026; draft-klrc-aiagent-auth-03, July 2026) trails RJB priority by roughly three months.
- **Remaining gap:** Universal and explicit across every source in this cluster: no identifiability condition attaches to any of the authorization gates. The gate answers "was this signed/delegated/confirmed by the right layer" but never "is the signer's causal contribution traceable" or "does the widened authority remain within an identifiable region." This is the single most load-bearing and most consistently unclosed gap in the entire corpus.

### World-model / latent-space identifiability (P3, TR-LSPD-1.0)
- **Convergence type:** Independent parallel, accumulating within a single active sub-field (the JEPA/predictive-representation identifiability line) rather than across unrelated fields.
- **Convergence depth:** Moving from Structural (Run 12–13 identifiability conditions) toward **Formal** at Run 14 — the certificate-gate protocol is a precise, reproducible experimental methodology with quantified results (probe R², decodability under reweighting, real-robot validation across 4,258 episodes).
- **RJB priority position:** P3/TR-LSPD-1.0 (April 2026) predates the cluster's strongest results (Run 14 item, v1 29 July 2026) by roughly three months. This is the same priority margin as the execution-gate cluster, suggesting a general ~3-month field-lag pattern relative to RJB deposit dates across at least two independent clusters.
- **Remaining gap:** The authors explicitly and repeatedly disclaim structural/classical identifiability, scoping claims as operational and objective-relative. They also make no governance or accountability claim of any kind. The gap is therefore double: (a) narrower formal scope than P3's ontological claim, and (b) no connection drawn to authorization or accountability architecture at all — which is precisely the connection RJB's Run 14 synthesis names as "the ABCF claim."

### Agent authority-chain / delegation topology (P3, TR-SA-1.1)

## Section 7 — Research positioning summary

**1. Where the field is moving relative to ABCF-v1.** Across fourteen runs and ten weeks, the field has moved from diagnosing an accountability problem to actively building two separate halves of ABCF's architecture — from opposite directions, without contact with each other or with RJB. On the authorization side, standards bodies (IETF WIMSE), commercial identity-governance research (Tallam), and embodied-agent robotics (Governable Individuals) have independently converged on the same structural move: authority may widen only through a rooted, signed act, and apparent consent or apparent behavior does not by itself constitute authorization. On the identifiability side, a distinct research community working on JEPA-class world models has, across three consecutive runs, moved from stating identifiability conditions to formally demonstrating a case where a causally-present, empirically-certified parameter is acquired by no tested predictive objective. Neither community engages the other's territory. RJB's Run 14 synthesis names this precisely: "the uncolonized territory is the join." That join — connecting a rooted-authorization gate to an identifiability precondition on what that gate can verify — remains, after fourteen runs, entirely unoccupied by anyone but ABCF.

**2. Concepts most at risk of independent convergence in the next 1–3 months.** The execution-gate/rooted-authorization cluster remains the highest-risk zone, and the risk has sharpened rather than eased: WIMSE's normative language now explicitly denies authority standing to unrooted confirmation, and Governable Individuals installs a near-identical gate from an unrelated field. If either line attaches even a coarse identifiability or traceability requirement to its existing gate — which several sources now flag as an open design question rather than an unconsidered one (e.g., draft-klrc-aiagent-auth-03's own §10.1 and §10.7 notes) — that would materially close the gap without RJB involvement. *This is a convergence-risk observation, not an IP/prior-art finding — that determination belongs to Layer 2.*

**3. Concepts structurally ahead with no visible convergence.** The ontological rank-deficiency characterization of structural indeterminacy (SLT/SI) remains untouched after fourteen runs and roughly fifteen independent sources approaching the attribution-limit problem from formal, legal, judicial, standards, and now legislative angles. P4 (economic domain) and P7 (Swiss/recognition neutrality) remain completely uncolonized after fourteen consecutive runs — the strongest and most repeatable null results in the corpus, now roughly three times the run-count of the Run 3 finding. P8 came off its own eight-run null this scope, but on a single data point that itself only reads as P8-relevant through RJB's own interpretive lens (the source makes no such claim) — it should not yet be treated as field convergence.

**4. Highest-priority research actions visible from the corpus.**
   (a) The join between the authorization cluster and the identifiability cluster — both now mature, both moving fast, neither connected — is the single clearest positioning opportunity the corpus has produced in fourteen runs. A short framing piece explicitly connecting Governable Individuals-style authority gating to certificate-gated identifiability limits would occupy ground that appears, on current evidence, genuinely unclaimed.
   (b) P4/P7 remain the cleanest priority/defensive-publication territory in the corpus, now with a fourteen-run confirmed-null base rather than five.
   (c) The Audit Illusion pattern has moved from a theoretical prediction (Runs 1–10) to a materializing legal fact: two EU instruments (Reg. 2026/1755, revised PLD) now converge on 2027 as the point at which compliance documentation will be tested as causal evidence in adversarial proceedings. This is a live, dated opportunity to have citation-ready framing in place before the first ruling that treats a compliance file as proof of decision integrity.
   (d) The companion technical report to Governable Individuals (announced, not yet located) is a near-term watch item that could materially change the strength of the authority-side convergence finding — worth checking before any near-term positioning decision that relies on that item's current (perspective-paper) status.
