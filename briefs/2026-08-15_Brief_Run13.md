## Item 1 — WIMSE at IETF 126: AI Agent Auth draft moves to Working Group adoption call; three WGLCs issued

### PART A

1. **Title:** WIMSE @ IETF 126: Meeting summary and follow-ups (chairs' announcement)
2. **Source:** IETF WIMSE mailing list (chairs Pieter Kasselman and Justin Richer); IETF 126 meeting materials
3. **Publication date:** ~30 July 2026 (chairs' summary); meeting held 20 July 2026
4. **Link:** https://mailarchive.ietf.org/arch/msg/wimse/Ol8vZ84pQP-Gz4gHEZEawKoXrxE/
5. **Jurisdiction / institution:** IETF WIMSE working group (international standards body)
6. **One-sentence factual summary:** Following the IETF 126 WIMSE session, the chairs announced Working Group Last Calls on three core workload-identity drafts (Mutual TLS, Workload Identifier, Workload-to-Workload HTTP Signatures) and a forthcoming Working Group Call for Adoption for the AI Agent Authentication and Authorization draft (draft-klrc-aiagent-auth-03, authored by Defakto, AWS, Zscaler, Ping Identity, OpenAI, Okta).
7. **Why selected:** Status escalation of the strongest continuing convergence vector in the corpus. `[PROXIMITY SIGNAL — RJB PUBLICATION: P3/TR-SA-1.1]` — the draft moving toward WG adoption encodes delegation-of-authority from user/system to agent with preserved principal context in authorization decisions and audit trails; adoption would give the rooted-delegation architecture WG-document standing.
8. **Relevant ABCF components:** rooted human authorization, authorization acts, provenance chains, decision traceability, containment boundaries
9. **Relevance level:** Direct relevance. ABCF is not mentioned in the source; relevance is architectural.

### PART B

**1. Coherence:** Fully coherent within the ABCF frame. The draft's core requirement — that when an agent acts on behalf of a user or system, authority must be explicitly delegated and the principal's context preserved into authorization decisions and audit trails — is the protocol-level expression of authority originating from an identifiable principal rather than from the agent itself.

**2. Alignment:** Aligned. WG adoption (if it occurs) moves the delegation architecture from individual-submission status into the WG's chartered output stream. Draft-03 (6 July 2026) also mandates that the LLM must never hold agent or downstream credentials — a containment boundary between the reasoning component and the execution-credential layer.

**3. Infringement or tension:** No direct tension. Standing limitation from prior runs persists: the draft treats delegation and audit as procedural/cryptographic requirements; it does not impose an identifiability condition on the delegation (nominal vs. substantive authority distinction is absent). Commentary in the practitioner literature (RockCyber, March 2026) already noted the authorization layer "stops at the token boundary."

- **Known facts:** IETF 126 WIMSE session held 20 July 2026; chairs announced three WGLCs and a forthcoming adoption call for draft-klrc-aiagent-auth; draft-03 posted 6 July 2026 with authors from six identity/AI vendors; a contributor (Rampalli) volunteered to join the draft work.
- **Inferences (marked):** *Inference:* an adoption call announced by chairs after in-meeting discussion usually succeeds; agent auth is likely to become a chartered WIMSE deliverable in H2 2026. *Inference:* WG adoption raises the likelihood that WIMSE — not OAuth WG or DIF — becomes the venue where the field's delegation vocabulary is fixed.
- **Unknowns:** Whether the adoption call has formally issued or concluded as of this run date; whether the layered chain/root separation from the Rampalli mapping (Run 12, Item 1) will be folded into the adopted document or remain a parallel record.
- **ABCF classification:** Coherent but incomplete.

## Item 2 — EU AI Act: Commission GPAI enforcement powers and Article 50 transparency duties activate 2 August 2026

### PART A

1. **Title:** EU AI Act Enforcement Phase Begins
2. **Source:** Wilson Sonsini client alert; Help Net Security; European Commission GPAI guidelines page; artificialintelligenceact.eu enforcement explainer
3. **Publication date:** 2–4 August 2026 (event date 2 August 2026)
4. **Link:** https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/eu-ai-act-enforcement-phase-begins.html
5. **Jurisdiction / institution:** European Union — European Commission / EU AI Office; national market surveillance authorities
6. **One-sentence factual summary:** On 2 August 2026 the Commission's AI Office became entitled to exercise investigation and enforcement powers over GPAI model providers (documentation requests, model access for evaluation, corrective measures, fines up to €15M or 3% of worldwide turnover), and Article 50 transparency duties (AI-interaction disclosure, machine-readable provenance marking of generated content, deepfake labeling) became enforceable by national authorities.
7. **Why selected:** The corpus has tracked this date since Run 11 (Omnibus); it has now landed. This is the largest jurisdiction converting AI-output provenance marking and model-access-for-evaluation from obligation-on-paper into an active enforcement regime. No RJB proximity flag — provenance marking here is content-level, not causal-attribution-level.
8. **Relevant ABCF components:** institutional authorization, provenance chains, model audit trails, admissibility checks (content-marking as a weak admissibility signal), governance authority
9. **Relevance level:** Direct relevance (regulatory milestone). ABCF is not mentioned in the source; relevance is architectural.

### PART B

**1. Coherence:** Coherent. An enforcement gate opening — the Commission can now compel documentation and model access — instantiates institutional authority exercising intervention capacity it previously held only nominally.

**2. Alignment:** Partially aligned. Machine-readable content provenance and mandatory AI-interaction disclosure strengthen the observable-provenance layer. The Commission's model-evaluation power is a bounded institutional intervention capacity beyond rollout-only observation — a limited, real expansion of the admissible intervention set for one regulator.

**3. Infringement or tension:** Structural gap ABCF names: the regime enforces documentation, marking, and disclosure — all observable-layer instruments. Nothing in the activated powers requires or tests causal identifiability of model contribution to downstream decisions. The Audit Illusion risk (P1/P5) transfers directly: compliance documentation produced under this regime will predictably be treated downstream (courts, procurement) as causal evidence of decision integrity, which it is not.

- **Known facts:** Enforcement powers effective 2 August 2026; fines up to €15M/3%; Article 50 enforceable by national MSAs; high-risk obligations deferred (Dec 2027 / Aug 2028) by the June 2026 Omnibus; pre-Aug-2025 models have until 2 August 2027.
- **Inferences (marked):** *Inference:* first Commission information requests to GPAI providers are likely within months; how the AI Office operationalizes "model access for evaluation" will define the de facto probe regime for the EU.
- **Unknowns:** Whether any enforcement action has actually been opened as of this run date; the technical scope of model-access evaluations.
- **ABCF classification:** Coherent but incomplete.

## Item 3 — On the Identifiability of Controlled World Models (§3b)

### PART A

1. **Title:** On the Identifiability of Controlled World Models
2. **Source:** arXiv preprint 2607.22430 (Zhang et al.)
3. **Publication date:** 24 July 2026
4. **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22430
5. **Jurisdiction / institution:** Academic (preprint)
6. **One-sentence factual summary:** The paper establishes a joint identifiability theory for action-conditioned (controlled) JEPA-class world models with Gaussian latent states, showing that recovery of both latent states and controlled dynamics requires two policy-dependent conditions — spectral separation (representation identifiability) and non-degenerate conditional action variation (transition identifiability) — and proves counterfactual prediction error amplifies inversely with the weakest action-excitation margin.
7. **Why selected:** §3b vector. `[PROXIMITY SIGNAL — RJB PUBLICATION: P3]` — matched concepts: identifiability conditions as preconditions for trusting latent-space causal structure; counterfactual (interventional) recovery failing under insufficient action variation, which is the behavior-policy analogue of rank deficiency in the admissible intervention set. The paper formalizes, inside the world-model program, that predictive training under a limited action regime leaves controlled dynamics confounded — predictive success ≢ causal recoverability.
8. **Relevant ABCF components:** causal identifiability, causal non-identifiability, attribution indeterminacy (latent-space)
9. **Relevance level:** Direct relevance to the §3b competitive/positioning vector. ABCF is not mentioned in the source; relevance is architectural.
10. *(No standards status — academic item.)*

### PART B

**1. Coherence:** Fully coherent. The result is structurally parallel to L-SLT's setup: whether causal contrasts (here, action effects) are recoverable depends on properties of the intervention/action distribution actually exercised, not on predictive loss. "Limited conditional action variation" confounding state evolution with action effects is the policy-side expression of an intervention set of insufficient rank.

**2. Alignment:** Aligned in the corroboration sense: a second consecutive run in which the world-model research program itself formalizes conditions under which its models fail to recover causal structure despite predictive competence.

**3. Infringement or tension:** None as a claim; the paper makes no governance claim. Watch condition from Run 12 persists and sharpens: as identifiability results accumulate inside the program (LeJEPA static case → now controlled/counterfactual case), the popularization risk shifts from "prediction is enough" to "identifiability is now proven, therefore world models are governance-grade" — eliding that the conditions are restrictive (Gaussian latents, specified policy classes) and policy-dependent.

**JEPA-class gap assessment:** The item does not claim predictive success suffices for governance; it demonstrates the opposite within a restricted regime, and quantifies counterfactual error amplification when action excitation is weak. It arrives independently at a claim structurally consistent with P3's core distinction. No engagement with governance framing; no citation of the RJB corpus found.

- **Known facts:** Submitted 24 July 2026; proves joint (representation + transition) identifiability under two named policy-dependent conditions; proves counterfactual error bound scaling inversely with the action-excitation margin; validated in four nonlinear settings; builds on Klindt et al.'s LeJEPA identifiability result.
- **Inferences (marked):** *Inference:* the identifiability line inside the JEPA program is now a sustained sub-field (Balestriero 2025 → Klindt et al. 2026 → generalization theory 2606.27014 → this paper), not a one-off.
- **Unknowns:** Peer-review status; whether any of these authors will extend toward governance/accountability framing.
- **ABCF classification:** Coherent and aligned.

**Housekeeping note (closes Run 12 §6 gap):** The LeJEPA identifiability primary source is confirmed as arXiv:2605.26379, "When Does LeJEPA Learn a World Model?", Klindt et al., submitted 25 May 2026 — Run 12 had assessed it only via secondary coverage.

## Item 4 — Securing Agentic AI: From Per-Action Checks to Trajectory Assurance

### PART A

1. **Title:** Securing Agentic AI: From Per-Action Checks to Trajectory Assurance
2. **Source:** arXiv preprint 2608.01558 (Lotfi, Shanto, Karim, Bertino); accepted to ACM AI Leadership Summit 2026, Visionary Track
3. **Publication date:** 3 August 2026
4. **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01558
5. **Jurisdiction / institution:** Academic (Purdue-affiliated group; Bertino is a leading database/security researcher)
6. **One-sentence factual summary:** A roadmap paper arguing that agent safety is determined by trajectory-level consistency with system invariants rather than per-action correctness — sequences of individually permissible actions may collectively violate system-level constraints — and that security must become a verifiable property of the architectures, protocols, and runtimes governing agent behavior rather than an optional guidance layer.
7. **Why selected:** Escalation of the execution-gate cluster from per-action gating to trajectory-level admissibility, from a senior mainstream security author in a leadership-track venue. `[PROXIMITY SIGNAL — RJB PUBLICATION: P1]` — matched concepts: containment boundaries as verifiable structural properties (not guidance); the paper's "behavioral containment" problem is the composition failure that per-action execution gates cannot detect — an admissibility question defined over the trajectory, not the act.
8. **Relevant ABCF components:** containment boundaries, execution gates, admissibility checks, runtime governance, decision traceability, provenance chains
9. **Relevance level:** Direct relevance. ABCF is not mentioned in the source; relevance is architectural.

### PART B

**1. Coherence:** Coherent. The trajectory-composition problem — each action individually admissible, the sequence collectively violating invariants — is a containment-boundary statement: admissibility is not closed under composition, so gate architecture defined per-action is structurally insufficient.

**2. Alignment:** Aligned in framing: "security must become a verifiable property of the architectures, protocols, and runtimes" is the anti-guidance, structural-enforcement position ABCF takes on governance generally. It also names provenance, accountability, and end-to-end observability as *open* problems rather than solved-by-logging — a refusal of the Audit Illusion at the field-roadmap level.

**3. Infringement or tension:** None as stated. Gap ABCF addresses: the paper poses trajectory assurance as a verification problem but does not pose the attribution question — who authorized the trajectory, and whether causal contribution along it is identifiable. Verifiable containment without rooted authorization governs the *what*, not the *on whose authority*.

- **Known facts:** Posted 3 August 2026; 6 pages; accepted to ACM AI Leadership Summit 2026 Visionary Track; identifies behavioral containment as "perhaps the most fundamental challenge"; treats supply-chain integrity, provenance, accountability, observability as largely open.
- **Inferences (marked):** *Inference:* Bertino's stature makes this a field-agenda-setting artifact; "trajectory assurance" is a plausible candidate term to organize the next wave of execution-gate work.
- **Unknowns:** Whether the group has a technical follow-on in progress; degree of uptake of the "trajectory assurance" framing.
- **ABCF classification:** Coherent but incomplete.
