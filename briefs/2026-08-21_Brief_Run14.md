## Item 1 — draft-klrc-aiagent-auth-03: adoption call not concluded; draft adds a normative separation of user confirmation from authorization

### PART A

1. **Title:** AI Agent Authentication and Authorization, draft-klrc-aiagent-auth-03
2. **Source:** IETF Datatracker (primary document text)
3. **Publication date:** 6 July 2026 (revision -03); document state verified 21 August 2026
4. **Link:** https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-klrc-aiagent-auth/
5. **Jurisdiction / institution:** IETF (individual submission; WIMSE-adjacent)
6. **Factual summary:** The draft remains an individual Internet-Draft at revision -03 with no stream defined and IESG state "I-D Exists"; the WG Call for Adoption announced at IETF 126 has not, as of this run, produced an adopted `draft-ietf-wimse-*` document.
7. **Why selected:** Closes a Run 13 open question on adoption-call outcome, and the -03 text (not previously examined at source level — Run 13 assessed this cluster from mailing-list state only) contains normative language separating in-session user confirmation from authorization. `[PROXIMITY SIGNAL — RJB PUBLICATION: P1, P3/TR-SA-1.1]` — CVG separation invariant (VALID ≢ MANDATE); rooted delegation; credential containment boundary.
8. **Relevant ABCF components:** explicit human authorization; authorization acts; containment boundaries; execution gates; admissibility checks; decision traceability; model audit trails
9. **Relevance level:** Direct relevance

### PART B

**1. Coherence:** Full. The document is an authorization architecture: it locates authority in an authorization server, requires delegation to be carried in verifiable grants, and defines what may and may not be treated as authorization.

**2. Alignment:** Three provisions are ABCF-aligned in substance:

- **§10.7 (Human in the Loop).** The draft states that an agent framework's in-execution user confirmation does not by itself constitute authorization, that such confirmation must be bound to a verifiable grant issued by the authorization server, and that local UI confirmation alone must not be treated as sufficient authorization. In ABCF terms this is a containment boundary stated normatively: an apparent-consent surface is denied authority standing unless it is converted into an explicit authorization act at the rooted authority. It is the closest thing in a live standards document to the CVG separation invariant.
- **§8 (Credential Provisioning).** The LLM must not have access to agent credentials or to credentials needed for tool and service access. This is a separation between the reasoning component and the authority-bearing artefact — structurally a chain/root separation, motivated in the draft by prompt-injection risk rather than by authority theory.
- **§11 (Monitoring, Observability, Remediation).** Audit records must include the authenticated agent identifier, the delegated subject where present, the authorization decision, the posture state influencing it, and remediation events; deployments should be able to reconstruct a complete execution chain.

**3. Infringement or tension:** Two gaps, both acknowledged in the document itself:

- **§10.1 (Agent Mission).** The translation of a natural-language mission into authorization requirements is explicitly out of scope. This is the causal step between human intent and the scope of granted authority — precisely the step ABCF treats as governance-critical. The architecture roots authority in an authorization server but does not govern how the requested scope was derived.
- **§10.7 note.** The draft records that CIBA accounts only for client initiation and maps poorly to cases requiring user confirmation mid-execution, and that further design work may be needed. The authorization-act architecture is therefore complete at task boundaries and incomplete during execution — the interval in which an agent's admissible action set actually evolves.
- No identifiability condition attaches anywhere. Authorization is gated on identity, credential possession, posture, and policy — never on whether the delegated subject's contribution to the resulting action could be traced. §11's reconstruction requirement is observable-layer: it records what was decided and by whom, not what the human's causal contribution was.

**Known facts:** Revision -03 dated 6 July 2026; authors from Defakto Security, AWS, Zscaler, Ping Identity, OpenAI, Okta; document is an individual I-D with no stream defined; §10.7 contains the confirmation/authorization separation in normative language; §10.1 declares mission-to-authorization translation out of scope; §8 prohibits LLM access to credentials.

**Inferences:** The adoption call announced at IETF 126 (20 July 2026) appears announced but not concluded, or concluded without a datatracker state change as of this run. The -03 revision history shows editorial and clarification work, not restructuring for WG adoption. The §10.7 language is more likely a response to MCP-style confirmation UX patterns than to any authority-theoretic argument.

**Unknowns:** Whether the adoption call formally issued and its outcome; whether the Rampalli layered chain/root mapping noted in Run 12 has entered the document (it does not appear in -03); whether any identifiability or attribution requirement is under discussion on the list.

**ABCF classification:** Coherent but incomplete

## Item 2 — Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1755: procedural rules for GPAI proceedings, including pre-proceedings interim measures

### PART A

1. **Title:** Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1755 of 20 July 2026
2. **Source:** EUR-Lex (primary); secondary practitioner analysis (Taylor Wessing, August 2026)
3. **Publication date:** 20 July 2026; in force 10 August 2026
4. **Link:** https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=OJ%3AL_202601755
5. **Jurisdiction / institution:** European Union — Commission / AI Office
6. **Factual summary:** The Regulation lays down procedural rules for Commission proceedings against GPAI model providers, including the opening of proceedings, access to file, confidentiality treatment, a five-year limitation period for fines, the appointment of independent experts to conduct model evaluations, and the power to order interim measures before proceedings are opened where there is urgency due to risk of serious damage.
7. **Why selected:** The first procedural instrument operationalizing the enforcement powers that Run 13 recorded as activating on 2 August 2026 — it converts a standing power into a defined procedure with named actors and evidentiary thresholds. `[PROXIMITY SIGNAL — RJB PUBLICATION: P1, P8]` — bounded probe expansion under authority activation; authority attaching before explanation is resolved.
8. **Relevant ABCF components:** institutional authorization; governance authority; execution gates; admissibility checks; provenance chains; decision traceability
9. **Relevance level:** Direct relevance
10. **Standards status:** Published; in force

### PART B

**1. Coherence:** Full. This is an authority architecture: it defines who may act, on what evidentiary basis, through what procedure, with what expansion of investigatory reach.

**2. Alignment:** Two structurally notable features:

- **Article 92 evaluation access, executed through appointed independent experts.** The Commission may appoint independent experts to conduct evaluations on its behalf. This is an authority-activated, bounded expansion of the observable set: the regulator's ordinary position is rollout-only (documentation, notifications, market observation); the evaluation power temporarily admits interventions — model access, adversarial testing — that are not otherwise available. Structurally this is the shape of a probe expansion executed by a named delegate under an explicit act. What is absent is any contraction guarantee or probe-execution-log analogue: nothing in the instrument requires the effective admissible set to be reconstructible after the fact, or restored.
- **Procedural definiteness.** Opening of proceedings, access to file, and limitation periods make the authority act datable and reviewable, which is the minimum condition for authority to be traceable to an institution rather than diffused into supervision.

**3. Infringement or tension:**

- **Interim measures on prima facie evidence, before proceedings are opened.** The Commission may order interim measures — notably preventing a model being made available — on urgency grounds prior to opening proceedings. This is authority attaching to a contested causal explanation before the explanation has been resolved. Under P8's sequencing invariant (recognition precedes explanation precedes authorization) this is a recognized structural risk, not an anomaly: urgency regimes exist precisely because the alternative is to wait for identifiability that may never arrive. The observation is not that the provision is wrong but that it is a live instance of the failure mode recognition neutrality is designed to name.
- **Audit Illusion surface expansion continues.** Run 13 flagged that the enforcement regime would become the largest live generator of observable-layer compliance artefacts. The procedural regulation confirms the direction: the file, the documentation, the evaluation record. The instrument governs what evidence must be produced and how it is handled; it does not address whether that evidence can bear the causal weight enforcement decisions will place on it.

**Known facts:** Regulation dated 20 July 2026, in force 10 August 2026; provides for interim measures by decision before opening proceedings on urgency grounds; permits appointment of independent experts for evaluations under Article 167 of Regulation (EU, Euratom) 2024/2509; sets confidentiality and access-to-file rules; five-year limitation period for fines. Chapter V obligations applicable since 2 August 2025; enforcement powers since 2 August 2026; pre-August-2025 models to comply by 2 August 2027.

**Inferences:** No enforcement action against a named provider has been detected in this run. The procedural build-out suggests preparation rather than imminent action. The independent-expert mechanism implies the Commission does not intend to hold evaluation capacity entirely in-house — creating a delegation chain from institution to expert whose authority basis is worth tracking.

**Unknowns:** Whether any proceeding has been opened; how "prima facie evidence" will be construed in practice; whether independent-expert appointments will be published; whether evaluation methodology will be disclosed.

**ABCF classification:** Coherent but incomplete

## Item 3 — What Can Latent World Models Know? A certificate-gated identifiability map with a certified-recoverable blind region (§3b)

### PART A

1. **Title:** What Can Latent World Models Know? Physical Parameter Identifiability in Multimodal Predictive Representations
2. **Source:** arXiv:2607.27017 (Tan, Xu, Tao, Hong, Feng, Du — NYU / Carnegie Mellon / Columbia)
3. **Publication date:** v1 29 July 2026; v2 circa early August 2026
4. **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27017
5. **Jurisdiction / institution:** Academic preprint (US)
6. **Factual summary:** Using a controlled environment in which hidden physical parameters are invisible in rendering, the authors first certify each parameter as recoverable from raw observations, then measure whether action-conditioned JEPA-class latents acquire it — finding that prediction targets rather than inputs decide what is retained, and that one certified-recoverable parameter is acquired by none of the prediction objectives tested.
7. **Why selected:** The strongest §3b result to date on the separation between predictive competence and recoverable causal content, and the first to make an identifiability certificate a precondition on making any claim at all. `[PROXIMITY SIGNAL — RJB PUBLICATION: P3 (TR-LSPD-1.0), P1]` — Representation Indeterminacy; Abstraction Collapse; JEPA-class governance gap; fail-closed on identifiability; probe-set dependence; intervention-side restoration.
8. **Relevant ABCF components:** causal identifiability; causal non-identifiability; attribution indeterminacy; admissibility checks; execution gates
9. **Relevance level:** Direct relevance

### PART B

**1. Coherence:** Full, and unusually precise. Four elements map onto ABCF objects:

- **The certificate gate.** No claim about a model is made until a probe on raw observations has certified the parameter recoverable. The stated purpose is to separate environment defects from representation defects and both from instrument defects, and thereby to license the causal language used. This is an identifiability precondition on interpretation — a fail-closed gate on causal claims, arrived at as methodology rather than as governance.
- **The blind region.** Drag carries a recoverability certificate of 0.89 from raw observations yet plateaus near 0.13 in the latent under every deterministic prediction objective tested, and the model's own predictions on that parameter are beaten by an estimator assuming the population median. Observable-level recoverability is intact; latent acquisition is not. This is the structural shape of Representation Indeterminacy.
- **The insufficiency of more of the same.** A thirtyfold reweighting toward the blind parameter raises decodability slightly while degrading functional use and the acquired parameters; the paper's summary is that objective structure decides what can be learned and data decides only how well. More evidence of the same kind does not restore what is missing.
- **Intervention-side restoration.** Two things move the blind parameter: a supervised system-identification head on the same trunk, and a change of sensed coordinate that linearizes the parameter's trace. Both are changes to what is asked of the system, not to how much is observed. This is the composition-theorem shape — identifiability restored by changing the admissible intervention set.

**2. Alignment:** Strongly corroborating for P3. The paper independently establishes, in the JEPA program's own terms and on the JEPA program's own substrate, that predictive success does not entail recovery of causally relevant latent content, and that the gap is a property of the objective class rather than a data deficiency. It also imports the probing literature's central caution — that probe results reflect probe capacity as much as representation content — which is the same dependence on the available probe set that TR-SA-1.1's Silent Directions construction turns on.

**3. Infringement or tension:** None. Two honest limits must be recorded:

- The authors explicitly scope their claims as operational and objective-relative, and expressly disclaim classical structural identifiability in the system-identification sense. Their statements hold under the tested models, coordinates, horizons, and objectives. This is a narrower claim than ontological indeterminacy, and should not be reported as a structural-indeterminacy result.
- The paper makes no governance or accountability claim of any kind. Its relevance is architectural, not doctrinal. ABCF is not mentioned in the source.

**Known facts:** Certificate-gated protocol requires recoverability certification before model claims; stiffness enters the latent only when touch is a prediction target (probe R² ~0.40–0.62) and not when merely fused as input (~ −0.02); drag holds a 0.89 certificate but plateaus near 0.13 across six families of prediction-side mechanisms, both probe families, and a pixel-reconstruction control; a supervised head on the same trunk reaches 0.45; a log-speed coordinate raises the linear certificate from near zero to 0.52 and the readout to 0.33; the mechanism proposed is conditional-mean collapse under squared-error point prediction; real-robot validation across two embodiments and 4,258 episodes reproduces the mechanism set.

**Inferences:** The stated attribute taxonomy — fast parameters acquired as needed, slow parameters only through their linear trace, slow ratio-type parameters not at all — reads as a candidate structural characterization of which causal directions a predictive objective can carry. Whether that hardens into a limit result depends on the discriminating test the authors name (objectives whose optimum is not a conditional mean), which is outside the scope of this paper.

**Unknowns:** Whether belief-state or episode-latent objectives escape the blind region; whether the taxonomy generalizes beyond the parameter–sensor pairs studied; whether any governance-adjacent group picks the certificate-gate methodology up.

**ABCF classification:** Coherent and aligned

## Item 4 — Governable Individuals: authority may widen only through signed lifecycle transitions

### PART A

1. **Title:** Governable Individuals: An Identity Layer for Embodied Agents That Keep Learning
2. **Source:** arXiv:2607.05463 (Qin, Luan, Yang, Li — Harbin Institute of Technology / Soochow University)
3. **Publication date:** v1 6 July 2026; v2 15 July 2026
4. **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05463
5. **Jurisdiction / institution:** Academic preprint (China)
6. **Factual summary:** The paper argues that governing a continually learning embodied agent means governing an individual rather than a model, that identifiers, activity logs and guardrails do not survive an agent that rewrites itself, and proposes an architecture in which competence may change without bound while authority, memory schema, embodiment rights and capability roster may widen only through signed lifecycle transitions updating a public identity commitment.
7. **Why selected:** Independent arrival at the capability/authority separation with an explicit signed-act requirement on authority expansion — the Integrator's Burden structure stated as an architecture. `[PROXIMITY SIGNAL — RJB PUBLICATION: P3 (TR-SA-1.1), P1]` — Integrator's Burden Corollary; Rooted Authority Invariant; nominal vs substantive authority; authority activation; monotone governance; rollout-evidence insufficiency.
8. **Relevant ABCF components:** explicit human authorization; authorization acts; governance authority; containment boundaries; execution gates; admissibility checks; runtime governance; provenance chains
9. **Relevance level:** Direct relevance

### PART B

**1. Coherence:** Full, and this is the closest architectural convergence in the corpus to date on the authority side.

**2. Alignment:** Four convergent elements:

- **Capability expansion is unbounded; authority expansion is gated.** Competence may change without limit; authority, memory schema, embodiment rights and the capability roster may widen only through a signed transition. ABCF's Integrator's Burden Corollary states the same asymmetry from the opposite direction — capability expansion without corresponding expansion of the integrator's probe set is a rooted-authority violation, requiring halt until a rank-expansion proof is supplied. The paper does not derive the proof obligation, but it installs the gate the obligation would attach to.
- **Admission by semantic effect, not by name.** The boundary contract admits by the effect of an action rather than by the identity of the calling routine; actions within the boundary are admitted autonomously and audited, while an acquired skill requesting secrets or access has a widening effect and is refused unless signed. This is fail-closed admissibility keyed to authority-widening, which is the correct gating variable under ABCF and not the one most execution-gate work selects.
- **A public identity commitment.** Authority state is externally legible and updated only by signed act — the nearest published analogue to a named-integrator topology with an inspectable authority edge.
- **Behavioural testing is declared insufficient.** The authors report that neither learned judgement nor behavioural testing was sufficient to carry the governance load on its own, and that the load-bearing layer must be structural. This is a rollout-evidence-insufficiency finding reached empirically.

**3. Infringement or tension:** No infringement. Two gaps:

- No identifiability condition. A transition is legitimate if signed; nothing requires that the signer's causal contribution to subsequent behaviour be traceable, or that the widened authority remain within an identifiable region. The gate is authorization-complete and attribution-empty — the same shape as the WIMSE cluster, arrived at from robotics rather than identity.
- The paper is a perspective paper; the companion technical report carrying the proofs and empirical evaluation is announced but, as of this run, not located on arXiv. The load-bearing claims are therefore not yet inspectable.

**Known facts:** v2 dated 15 July 2026; classified q-bio.NC; described in the listing as a perspective paper with a companion technical report to follow; the same group published a related capability-evolution architecture in April 2026 (arXiv:2604.07799) in which the agent's decision policy is never modified and a runtime layer blocks unsafe actions.

**Inferences:** The group's two papers together read as a deliberate separation of a mutable capability layer from a stable identity-and-authority layer — chain/root separation in an embodied setting. Whether the companion report attempts a formal result on authority containment under unbounded competence drift is the question that decides whether this becomes a proximity signal or a competitive one.

**Unknowns:** The content of the companion technical report and its posting date; whether the signed-transition scheme has any identifiability or probe-capacity requirement; whether the work has drawn standards-body attention.

**ABCF classification:** Coherent and aligned

## Item 5 — W3C: Agent Identity Registry Protocol Community Group; VC WG 2026 charter includes Confidence Method

1. **Title:** Agent Identity Registry Protocol Community Group (W3C); Verifiable Credentials Working Group Charter 2026
2. **Source:** W3C Community Groups; W3C VC WG charter
3. **Publication date:** CG proposed 22 April 2026, call for participation 24 April 2026; VC WG charter operating from April 2026
4. **Link:** https://www.w3.org/community/agent-identity/ ; https://w3c.github.io/vc-charter-2026/
5. **Jurisdiction / institution:** W3C
6. **Factual summary:** A W3C Community Group has been formed to develop specifications for AI agent credentials that bind agents to their controlling organizations and enable cross-organizational trust negotiation without prior bilateral agreements, with anticipated coordination across the W3C Credentials CG, DIF, the OpenID Foundation AIIM CG and the IETF WIMSE working group; separately the rechartered VC WG lists a Confidence Method specification intended to increase a verifier's confidence about the subject identified in a credential, targeted for Recommendation in September 2026.
7. **Why selected:** Closes a two-run coverage gap on W3C, and establishes that organizational binding of agent authority is now being pursued in a fourth venue with explicit cross-venue coordination.
8. **Relevant ABCF components:** named accountable human integrators; institutional authorization; provenance chains; decision traceability
9. **Relevance level:** Indirect relevance
10. **Standards status:** Community Group (pre-standardization); VC WG deliverables in Working Draft with Recommendation targets

### PART B

**1. Coherence:** Partial. Binding an agent to a controlling organization is a rooted-authority move, but the root is institutional rather than a named human integrator, and the mechanism is credential presentation rather than an authorization act.

**2. Alignment:** The organizational-binding requirement is the correct direction — it denies agents self-originating standing and requires authority to trace to an accountable entity. The Confidence Method deliverable is more interesting than its name suggests: it introduces a graded assurance dimension over subject identification, which is the first standards-track object in this cluster that treats identification as something admitting degrees rather than a binary. Whether that graduation ever attaches to anything causal is the open question.

**3. Infringement or tension:** Community Group output carries no standards standing, and coordination across four venues is announced rather than demonstrated. Run 13 noted that fragmentation across a very large agent-draft landscape would concentrate authority-vocabulary decisions in a few venues; this item is evidence of the concentration beginning, with WIMSE named as a coordination partner from outside the IETF. No identifiability or attribution condition appears in any of the material examined.

**Known facts:** CG originally proposed 2026-04-22 by Aaron Adolfo Grego; work described as taking inspiration from TrustLayer Foundation A.C. material without being constrained by it; coordination anticipated with W3C CCG, DIF, OpenID Foundation AIIM CG, IETF WIMSE WG; VC WG charter operating from April 2026 with Render Method and Confidence Method Recommendations targeted September 2026 and all non-tentative deliverables at Candidate Recommendation by July 2027; DID v1.1 reached Candidate Recommendation in March 2026.

**Inferences:** The CG's framing — cross-organizational trust without pre-existing bilateral agreements — implies a registry or discovery layer, which would make the organizational binding externally resolvable. That is a stronger provenance position than credential presentation alone, and worth watching.

**Unknowns:** Whether the CG has produced any draft; the substantive content of Confidence Method; whether the announced coordination with WIMSE is active.

## Item 6 — EU Revised Product Liability Directive: causation burden reversal, transposition deadline December 2026

### PART A

1. **Title:** Revised Product Liability Directive — transposition and AI causation presumptions
2. **Source:** Practitioner analyses (K&L Gates, March 2026; Legal Finance Expert, May 2026); academic comparative analysis (Frontiers in AI, June 2026)
3. **Publication date:** Analyses March–June 2026; transposition deadline December 2026
4. **Link:** https://www.klgates.com/AI-Product-Liability-The-Next-Wave-of-Litigation-3-27-2026
5. **Jurisdiction / institution:** European Union; Member States
6. **Factual summary:** The revised Directive treats software including AI systems as products, extends strict-liability concepts across the distribution chain, captures parties that substantially modify AI systems, and provides that where a claimant cannot access the information needed to prove a defect the evidentiary burden shifts toward the defendant; Member States must transpose by December 2026.
7. **Why selected:** A legal system responding to attribution difficulty by reallocating the burden rather than resolving identifiability — the mechanism P5 describes. `[PROXIMITY SIGNAL — RJB PUBLICATION: P5]` — liability sink; attribution systematically impracticable; admissible observation regime.
8. **Relevant ABCF components:** attribution indeterminacy; accountability gaps; decision traceability; institutional authorization
9. **Relevance level:** Direct relevance

### PART B

**1. Coherence:** Full. The Directive's disclosure-and-presumption machinery exists because the drafters accepted that claimants cannot establish causal contribution inside an AI system through ordinary proof.

**2. Alignment:** Partially aligned, in an important and easily missed way. The burden-reversal mechanism does not assert that causation has been established. It concedes that it may not be establishable by the party who bears the ordinary burden, and reallocates the cost of that indeterminacy. That is more honest than an audit regime that treats compliance documentation as causal evidence — it names the limit rather than papering it. Under P5's framing this is the legal system acting correctly on its own recognition that it operates within an admissible observation regime it cannot expand.

**3. Infringement or tension:** The allocation question is resolved; the causal question is not. Three consequences:

- **The indeterminacy is relocated, not removed.** A defendant required to disprove defect faces the same structural obstacle from the other side. Where attribution is genuinely indeterminate, burden reversal decides who loses rather than what happened. That is a legitimate policy choice, but it is a distributional resolution to a structural problem, and P5's liability-sink construction is exactly the shape it takes when the absorbing party is institutional.
- **Compliance documentation acquires evidentiary weight it may not bear.** Practitioner commentary reports plaintiffs' counsel already targeting AI Act compliance documentation in pre-litigation discovery. Combined with Item 2's expanding documentation regime, the two instruments together create a large corpus of observable-layer artefacts entering adversarial proceedings as proxies for causal evidence. This is the Audit Illusion operating at scale in the legal domain rather than the compliance domain.
- **"Substantial modification" as an attribution anchor.** Capturing parties that substantially modify an AI system assigns responsibility by intervention proximity rather than by traced causal contribution. Under ABCF this is attribution by locus rather than by identifiability.

**Known facts:** Directive classifies software including AI as products; captures substantial modifiers; provides disclosure obligations and presumptions where information asymmetry prevents proof; Member State transposition deadline December 2026; California AB 316 addresses "autonomy" defences with a similar refusal to allow autonomy to function as a categorical shield.

**Inferences:** The convergence of the December 2026 transposition deadline, the high-risk provisions phasing through 2027–2028, and the live GPAI enforcement regime means the first cases in which compliance artefacts are tested as causal evidence are likely to arise in 2027. That is the point at which the Audit Illusion becomes a litigated question rather than a theoretical one. ABCF is not mentioned in any source; relevance is architectural.

**Unknowns:** Member State transposition variation; how courts will treat the presumption where the defendant's own evidence is also structurally incapable of resolving causation; whether any decided case has yet turned on this.

**ABCF classification:** Coherent problem signal
