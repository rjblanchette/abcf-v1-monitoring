3. **Publication date:** May 2026 (arXiv postings); series drafts cited as 2026 working manuscripts through June–July 2026 downstream citations

5. **Jurisdiction / institution:** Independent research (Krti Tallam)
6. **One-sentence factual summary:** A cumulative series arguing that authorization must be treated as infrastructure: "execution envelopes" define a shared admission contract for backend AI execution requests, "fail-and-report" names a missing authorization primitive in which denied or partial authorization must produce an explicit typed report rather than silent degradation, and companion work defines four accountability constraints (traceability, auditability, controllability, recovery) enforced at named system seams including the human-operator/delegated-agent boundary.

Adjacent to the Fernandez atomic-decision-boundary line tracked since Run 11, but arrived at from the identity-governance side.
8. **Relevant ABCF component(s):** execution gates, admissibility checks, authorization acts, decision traceability, provenance chains
9. **Relevance level:** Direct relevance

### PART B
- **Coherence:** High — admission contracts, typed refusal reporting, and authorization propagation across the human→agent seam all sit inside the ABCF execution-gate and rooted-authorization frame.
- **Alignment:** Supports ABCF-compatible governance — treats authorization as a structural substrate ("receipt-bearing admission surfaces are part of the authorization substrate") rather than an access-control afterthought; Partial Evidence Bench additionally demonstrates that a system can enforce permissions correctly and still mislead its consumer about result completeness — an evidence-integrity failure mode adjacent to the Audit Illusion pattern, though framed operationally rather than as a causal-identifiability theorem.
- **Infringement/tension:** None as governance-sufficiency claim; worth watching as a rapidly-expanding independent operationalization of admission-gate architecture. Several series entries remain unpublished working drafts, so the cluster's final form is not yet fixed.
- **Known facts:** arXiv postings for Authorization Propagation and Partial Evidence Bench are live; Execution Envelopes, Fail-and-Report, and Machine-Speed Accountability circulate as cited working manuscripts; the series is already being cited by third parties (e.g., The Khipu Problem, arXiv:2606.12414). - **Unknowns:** Whether the working-draft entries will be deposited on arXiv in current form; whether Tallam engages any identifiability precondition for authorization or treats authorization purely as an infrastructure/security property.

## Item 4 — World-model identifiability cluster: certificate gates and JEPA identifiability conditions (§3b)

### PART A
1. **Title:** On the Identifiability of Controlled World Models; What Can Latent World Models Know? Physical Parameter Identifiability in Multimodal Predictive Representations; related: LeJEPA linear-identifiability result (Klindt et al., 2026)
2. **Source:** arXiv (2607.22430; 2607.27017); LeJEPA proof submitted to arXiv 25 May 2026 (secondary coverage: TechTimes, 31 May 2026)
3. **Publication date:** July 2026 (both primary papers); May 2026 (LeJEPA proof)
4. **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22430 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27017
5. **Jurisdiction / institution:** Academic research (multiple groups), building on LeCun/Meta FAIR LeJEPA line
6. **One-sentence factual summary:** A cluster of formal results on what JEPA-class latent world models can and cannot identify: joint identifiability conditions for controlled world models under Gaussian latent assumptions (spectral separation governing representation identifiability, a second condition governing transition identifiability); and an experimental framework in which every causal claim about a latent representation must first pass a "certificate gate" — a probe on raw observations certifying a parameter as recoverable before any statement about the model is licensed — with some physical parameters found to stay systematically absent from prediction-trained latents.
7. **Why it was selected:** `[PROXIMITY SIGNAL — RJB PUBLICATION: P3]` — this is the first substantive §3b return after five-plus empty runs on the vector. The certificate-gate design (no causal language licensed without a prior recoverability certificate) is structurally parallel to the L-SPD / semantic-anchoring / fail-closed pattern; the finding that parameters remain systematically absent from latents despite predictive competence is an empirical analogue of Abstraction Collapse / Representation Indeterminacy; and the identifiability-condition papers formalize precisely the predictive-success ≢ identifiability distinction P3 states — under restrictive (Gaussian, stationary) assumptions.
8. **Relevant ABCF component(s):** causal identifiability, representation indeterminacy, admissibility checks, epistemic calibration
9. **Relevance level:** Direct relevance

### PART B
- **Coherence:** High — the field is now producing formal identifiability conditions and gate-style epistemic discipline for exactly the latent-space regime P3 addresses.
- **Alignment:** Supports ABCF-compatible governance in structure, with a critical caveat: these results *restore* identifiability only under strong distributional assumptions (Gaussian latents, stationary additive-noise dynamics). Outside those assumptions the P3 gap stands; the papers do not claim otherwise.
- **Infringement/tension:** Watch — secondary coverage frames the LeJEPA proof as vindication of the world-model bet ("earns a formal proof"). If the restricted result is popularly read as "world models are identifiable, therefore governance-grade," that would be the claim P3 rebuts. The primary papers do not make that claim; the popularization pathway might.
- **JEPA-class gap assessment:** Neither primary paper claims predictive success is sufficient for governance; both engage the identifiability distinction directly — the certificate-gate paper operationalizes it. No item this run arrives at a claim ABCF formally rebuts; the cluster instead corroborates the distinction P3 formalized, in a restricted setting.
- **Known facts:** 2607.22430 leaves open identifiability outside the Gaussian regime; 2607.27017's probes are ridge regressions (linear availability only). ABCF is not mentioned in the sources; relevance is architectural.
- **Inferences:** The world-model program is internalizing identifiability as a first-class question roughly three months after P3's deposit — independent convergence, no citation found.
- **Unknowns:** Whether the certificate-gate methodology generalizes to intervention-relevant (not merely parameter-recovery) contrasts; whether Meta FAIR incorporates identifiability conditions into governance-facing messaging.
- **ABCF classification:** Coherent and aligned
