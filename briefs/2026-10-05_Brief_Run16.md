## Item 1 — *Amazon.com Services v. Perplexity AI* (9th Cir., 4 Aug 2026): the agent is a tool; the user is the accessor (backfill)

### PART A

1. **Title:** Amazon.com Services LLC v. Perplexity AI, Inc., Ninth Circuit decision on the CFAA and an agentic browser
2. **Source:** Wilson Sonsini data advisory (secondary, law-firm commentary); Enterprise DNA (secondary). The opinion text and the Troutman summary could not be retrieved (redirect loop).
3. **Publication date:** Decision 4 August 2026
4. **Link:** https://www.wsgrdataadvisor.com/2026/08/ninth-circuit-addresses-cfaa-and-agentic-ai-tools-in-groundbreaking-decision/
5. **Jurisdiction / institution:** United States, Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals
6. **Factual summary:** The Ninth Circuit vacated the preliminary injunction against Perplexity's Comet browser assistant. It held that the user, not Perplexity, "accesses" Amazon's computers when the assistant acts, and that the assistant is a tool rather than a person for CFAA purposes. Intent to access is attributed to the user because the assistant acts at the user's direction.
7. **Why selected:** It closes the Run 3 follow-up. Run 3 recorded the oral arguments (11 Jun 2026) and asked whether user delegation grounds agent authority. The decision answers that question for this statute. **No new flag.** The holding attributes responsibility by direction and control without requiring causal traceability, which §9.9 lists as a do-not-trigger condition. The Run 3 flag (P1/P2) stands as recorded.
8. **Relevant ABCF components:** human authorization; named accountable human; accountability gaps; attribution
9. **Relevance level:** Indirect relevance

### PART B

**1. Coherence:** High. It is the first federal appellate treatment recorded in the corpus of who is the responsible party when an AI agent acts. The answer roots responsibility in a human principal rather than in the agent or its developer.

**2. Alignment:** Rooting an agent's act in the directing human is consistent with ABCF's rooted human authorization. The court did not treat the agent as a locus of authority.

**3. Infringement or tension:** The attribution rests on a proxy, "at the user's direction", and not on any analysis of causal contribution. Whether a user who issues a high-level instruction has a causal role in a specific agent action is exactly the question ABCF treats as potentially indeterminate above a computable threshold. The ruling does not engage that question. It also sits beside the two German first-instance rulings recorded in Run 15 Item 4 (München I attributes AI Overviews content to the operator; Berlin II does not). Three courts now attribute the acts of an AI system by three different observable features: operator synthesis, operator influence over sources, and user direction. The statutes differ (defamation, trademark/UWG, CFAA), which explains part of the divergence.

**Known facts:** Case, court and decision date as stated. Injunction vacated. The user is the accessor. The agent is a tool. Intent is attributed to the user. The case was remanded for further proceedings, with potential human-user liability under the CFAA identified in one secondary report.

**Inferences:** The ruling makes the human principal the default attributable party for agentic acts under this statute. That is useful as a rooted-authority anchor. It also means the law has fixed the attribution before anyone has measured the user's causal contribution. *Speculative:* the question will return when an agent's act diverges materially from the instruction.

**Unknowns:** The opinion text and its reasoning on authorization. Whether the panel addressed delegation depth or multi-agent chains. Whether further review is sought. The scope of the remand.

**ABCF classification:** Coherent but incomplete

7. **Why selected:** §3a IETF pass, which also surfaced it as an unrecorded neighbour of the WIMSE line. `[PROXIMITY SIGNAL — RJB PUBLICATION: P1, P2]`:

8. **Relevant ABCF components:** execution gates; admissibility checks; containment boundaries; runtime governance; decision traceability
9. **Relevance level:** Direct relevance
10. **Standards status:** Draft (Independent Submission; not a WG item; Informational)

### PART B

**1. Coherence:** Full on the execution side. The draft frames accountability as an enforcement problem at the point of external effect.

**2. Alignment:**
- Authorization ("may this workflow proceed?") and effectuation verification ("may this exact consequence occur now?") are distinct functions.
- The governed operation must not become externally effective until verification succeeds (R1).
- Authority sits in the boundary, not in the model.

**3. Infringement or tension:**
- Per the extraction, the draft does **not** require a named human accountable party at the final boundary. Humans define permitted boundaries; infrastructure enforces them. That is authority without a rooted, named integrator, which is the structural requirement ABCF places on the chain.
- No attribution limit or identifiability condition appears. No reference to Tibebu or to any impossibility result was found.
- The problem is framed as cross-vendor semantic interoperability, not as governance policy. That is a scope choice, not a defect.

**Known facts:** Author, status, dates and expiry as stated. Requirement R1 as paraphrased. Authorization and boundary verification are distinguished. No Tibebu citation found in the extracted text.

**Inferences:** An independent submission carries less standing than a WG document (Run 15 Item 1). It still adds to the number of IETF texts that place authority at a gate and not in the model. *Speculative:* it may be a candidate for adoption into an existing WG, which cannot be assessed from the draft.

**Unknowns:** Date and content of -00. Whether any WG has been approached. Whether the draft addresses revocation or contraction. Extraction was summary-level, so the full text needs checking before Layer 1 relies on the R1 wording.

## Item 3 — Governed Individuation (arXiv:2607.04613, 6 Jul 2026): the Governable Individuals companion report (backfill)

### PART A

1. **Title:** Governed Individuation: Cryptographically Decoupling an Agent's Learning from Its Authority
2. **Source:** arXiv (HTML full text, primary). Companion to arXiv:2607.05463 (Governable Individuals; Run 14 Item 4).
3. **Publication date:** 6 July 2026 (v1)
4. **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04613
5. **Jurisdiction / institution:** Qin, Luan, Yang, Li (Harbin Institute of Technology; Soochow University)
6. **Factual summary:** The report proves a bound on the probability that a learning agent executes effects exceeding its authorized permission ceiling. A frozen cryptographic identity digest binds the agent to the ceiling, effects are evaluated by semantic effect and not by name, and the ceiling changes only through operator-signed transitions. Theorem 1 (Permission-Boundary Conservation) is conditional on a sound, conservative effect abstraction with bounded false-allow probability.
7. **Why selected:** It closes Run 15 open item (b) and Layer 1 Run 4 §8 vector 2. Run 14 stated that if the companion carried a formal containment result, the classification of Item 4 might need revisiting. `[PROXIMITY SIGNAL — RJB PUBLICATION: P3/TR-SA-1.1]`:
   - capability unbounded while authority widens only through a signed act (Integrator's Burden Corollary analogue)
   - authority rooted in an operator signature over an identity commitment (Rooted Authority Invariant analogue)
   - effect-based admission, with soundness of the effect abstraction as the load-bearing assumption (Representation Indeterminacy analogue, see Part B)
   **Convergence depth: Structural.** There is now a formal bound, not only an architectural statement.
8. **Relevant ABCF components:** explicit human authorization; authorization acts; containment boundaries; execution gates; admissibility checks
9. **Relevance level:** Direct relevance

### PART B

**1. Coherence:** Full. The report separates competence from authority and proves that the separation holds whatever happens to the agent's learned judgment, provided the verifier is sound.

**2. Alignment:** The result is a probabilistic containment guarantee tied to a signed human act. It supports the ABCF position that governance should not depend on the quality of the agent's own judgment.

**3. Infringement or tension:**
- **The assumption carries the weight.** Per the extraction, the authors call verifier soundness "the hard systems problem". They state that soundness is undefined for open action spaces. Whether an effect abstraction is faithful to the true causal effects of an action is the question ABCF's latent-space work treats as potentially indeterminate (a faithful-looking abstraction can hide contrast-relevant directions). The report does not address identifiability of the verifier's abstraction.
- Authorization-complete and attribution-empty, as in Run 14.

**Known facts:** Authors, date, abstract and Theorem 1 as stated, including its dependence on verifier soundness. The authors do not assume δ_A = 0 empirically. No identifiability restriction is noted.

**Inferences:** *For Layer 1:* the companion does supply a formal containment result, which is the condition Run 14 set for revisiting Item 4's classification (Coherent and aligned). On the evidence retrieved, "Coherent but incomplete" now fits the pair better, because the load-bearing assumption is exactly where ABCF places its unresolved question. That is an input for Layer 1, not a reclassification made here. The reclassification is RJ's decision.

**Unknowns:** The proof structure (extraction was summary-level). The size of the false-allow bound in the reported evaluation. Whether the effect tracer's thirty-two-attack evaluation generalizes. Whether either paper has been reviewed or published at a venue.

**ABCF classification:** Coherent but incomplete
