### Item 2 — IETF agent-delegation draft proliferation (execution-gate cluster) — carried, materially strengthened

**PART A**
1. **Title:** Multiple IETF Internet-Drafts on verifiable AI-agent delegation and authorization
2. **Source:** IETF datatracker (primary): draft-nelson-agent-delegation-receipts-10 (Jun 2026); draft-klrc-aiagent-auth-02 (Jun 2026); draft-prakash-aip-00 / draft-singla-agent-identity-protocol-00 (Mar–Apr 2026); OAuth WG list "delegation chain splicing" thread (Mar 2026)
3. **Publication date:** March–June 2026 (active revision)
4. **Link:** https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-nelson-agent-delegation-receipts/ ; https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-klrc-aiagent-auth/
5. **Jurisdiction / institution:** IETF (individual submissions; no formal standing yet)
6. **Factual summary:** A cluster of drafts is converging on multi-hop delegation with cryptographic scope attenuation, append-only provenance/completion records, human-in-the-loop confirmation, and chain-depth/scope-violation gates.
7. **Why selected:** `[PROXIMITY SIGNAL — RJB PUBLICATION: P1, P2, P3]` — Rooted Authority Invariant (path from root principal to executing node, each edge a valid delegation); Monotone Governance / scope-attenuation (A ⊆ A′); Deployment Gate / fail-closed (deny on PARENT_SCOPE_VIOLATION); PEL / AWAP analogue (signed completion records with result hash). ABCF not mentioned; relevance architectural.
8. **ABCF component(s):** execution gates; admissibility checks; authorization acts; named accountable human integrators; provenance chains; decision traceability
9. **Relevance level:** Direct relevance

**PART B**
- **Coherence:** High. These drafts are building the authorization-before-execution gate at protocol level.
- **Alignment:** Strong on structure. draft-klrc-aiagent-auth §9.7 (Human in the Loop) makes the authorization server able to require explicit out-of-band user confirmation for high-access requests — an execution-gate analogue. AIP completion blocks (status, result_hash, verification_status, signed by executor) are a provenance-packet analogue. Scope attenuation per hop is the Monotone Governance direction.
- **Infringement / tension:** Same structural gap as Item 1, at the protocol layer. Authority validity is established **topologically and cryptographically** (does a signed delegation chain exist? is scope contained?), not by **geometric identifiability** (is the delegated action's causal contribution identifiable under the integrator's probe set?). Per P3, topological connectivity (the Maffeis–Mitchell–Taly condition these drafts effectively encode) is necessary but not sufficient; identifiability along every path is the missing second condition. This is the convergence Layer 1 §7 flagged as "most at risk": the field may standardize an execution gate **without** the identifiability condition, partially absorbing ABCF's surface claim.
- **Known facts:** Draft contents, revision dates, WG thread topics.
- **Inferences:** The gate architecture is maturing faster than the standards-body (NIST/ISO) layer; protocol convergence may precede regulatory convergence.
- **Unknowns:** Whether any draft introduces an identifiability precondition rather than pure scope containment.
- **ABCF classification:** Coherent but incomplete (with rising convergence-risk salience)
