## Item 1 — WIMSE adopts the agent-auth draft: draft-ietf-wimse-aims-00 (15 Sep 2026)

### PART A

1. **Title:** AI Identity Management System, draft-ietf-wimse-aims-00
2. **Source:** IETF Datatracker (primary); `ietf-wg-wimse/draft-ietf-wimse-aims` GitHub (rename PR #148; release tag -00)
3. **Publication date:** 15 September 2026
4. **Link:** https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ietf-wimse-aims/
5. **Jurisdiction / institution:** IETF, WIMSE Working Group
6. **Factual summary:** The individual draft `draft-klrc-aiagent-auth` has been adopted as a WIMSE Working Group document under a new name, `draft-ietf-wimse-aims`. Revision -00 is dated 15 September 2026, its stream is IETF, its IESG state is "I-D Exists", and it formally replaces `draft-klrc-aiagent-auth`.
7. **Why selected:** It closes Run 14 open item (a) and Layer 1 §8 vector 7. The adoption call announced at IETF 126 has concluded with adoption. The normative provisions examined in Run 14 carry into the WG document unchanged in substance: §10.7 (confirmation is not authorization), §8 (LLM denied credential access), §10.1 (mission-to-authorization translation out of scope) and §11 (reconstruction of authorization context). Their standing has escalated from an individual submission to a WG-owned standards-track work item. `[PROXIMITY SIGNAL — RJB PUBLICATION: P1, P3/TR-SA-1.1]`: CVG Separation Invariant (VALID ≢ MANDATE); Rooted Authority Invariant (authority carried only by a verifiable grant from the authorization server); containment boundary between the reasoning component and the authority-bearing credential.
8. **Relevant ABCF components:** explicit human authorization; authorization acts; containment boundaries; execution gates; admissibility checks; decision traceability; model audit trails
9. **Relevance level:** Direct relevance
10. **Standards status:** Draft (WG document, pending WGLC)

### PART B

**1. Coherence:** Full. The document is an authorization architecture for agents. Authority is rooted in an authorization server, delegation travels as verifiable grants, and the document specifies what does not count as authorization.

**2. Alignment:** Carried forward from Run 14 and confirmed in the adopted text:
- **§10.7:** "Such interactions do not by themselves constitute authorization." Also: "The agent MUST NOT treat local UI confirmation alone as sufficient authorization." Confirmation must be translated into an OAuth authorization event, for example step-up via CIBA.
- **§8:** "The Large Language Model MUST NOT have access to an agent's credentials or to credentials that may be needed to access tools and services."
- **§11:** "Deployments MUST maintain sufficient monitoring and observability to reconstruct agent behavior and authorization context after execution." Invalidated cached authorization "MUST NOT continue to be used" after revocation or risk notification. This is a revocation-driven contraction rule.

**3. Infringement or tension:** Unchanged, and now held by the WG rather than by six authors:
- §10.1 keeps mission-to-authorization translation out of scope. The step between human intent and the scope of granted authority remains ungoverned.
- No identifiability condition attaches anywhere. §11 reconstruction is observable-layer: it shows who authorized what, not what the human's causal contribution was.
- The mid-execution authorization gap flagged in -03 (CIBA fits poorly for confirmation mid-task) is not resolved in -00 on the evidence retrieved.

**Known facts:** Revision -00 is dated 15 Sep 2026. WG state is "WG Document" and it replaces `draft-klrc-aiagent-auth`. The author set is unchanged (Defakto, AWS, Zscaler, Ping Identity, OpenAI, Okta). §10.7, §8 and §11 normative text is quoted above from the adopted document. The history appendix records added phishing-resistant authentication guidance and OAuth clarifications for agent clients.

**Inferences:** Adoption moves this text onto the path that ends in an RFC. Future changes to §10.7, §8 and §11 now require WG consensus rather than author discretion, so the provisions have become more durable. The rename from "AI Agent Authentication and Authorization" to "AI Identity Management System" frames the work as identity infrastructure. That may reduce the attention the WG gives to authority semantics over time. This inference is speculative.

**Unknowns:** A full -03 → -00 diff has not been run. Also unknown: whether any list thread proposes attribution or identifiability requirements; the WGLC timeline; whether the Rampalli layered chain/root mapping (Run 12) has been raised in the WG.

**ABCF classification:** Coherent but incomplete

## Item 2 — McCann "structural governance" cluster: Rice's theorem, coterminous governance, mechanized proofs (backfill, Apr–May 2026)

### PART A

1. **Title:** "The Two Boundaries: Why Behavioral AI Governance Fails Structurally" (arXiv:2604.27292), with companions "Mechanized Foundations of Structural Governance" (arXiv:2604.27289), "Algebraic Semantics of Governed Execution" (arXiv:2605.01032), "Effect-Transparent Governance for AI Workflow Architectures" (arXiv:2605.01030) and "Certified Purity for Cognitive Workflow Executors" (arXiv:2605.01037)
2. **Source:** arXiv (primary, HTML full text for 2604.27292); `mashin-live/governance-proofs` GitHub
3. **Publication date:** April 2026 (2604.xxxxx) and May 2026 (2605.xxxxx). Exact submission days were not retrieved.
4. **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27292 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27289 · https://github.com/mashin-live/governance-proofs
5. **Jurisdiction / institution:** Alan L. McCann, Mashin, Inc. (industry)
6. **Factual summary:** The paper argues from Rice's theorem that deciding whether an arbitrary Turing-complete program's effects comply with any non-trivial governance policy is undecidable, so behavioral governance cannot be complete. It proposes "coterminous governance" instead: code computes only pure directives, and every effect passes through a single governed boundary. The companion papers claim a machine-checked foundation (the paper cites 454 theorems in Rocq; the repository reports 548 Coq theorems, zero admitted lemmas).
7. **Why selected:** Layer 1 §8 vector 6 (new). It answers the Intent-Driven Computing question: this is the undecidability-grounded impossibility line, turned into an architecture. It was not previously recorded in the corpus. `[PROXIMITY SIGNAL — RJB PUBLICATION: P1, P2]`: behavioral/observational evidence insufficient for governance as a *structural* result rather than a policy observation (an Audit Illusion analogue, reached by computability rather than identifiability); a single mandatory effect gate as the governance locus (execution-gate / deployment-gate architecture); governance proved by construction rather than by monitoring.
8. **Relevant ABCF components:** execution gates; admissibility checks; runtime governance; containment boundaries; accountability incompleteness
9. **Relevance level:** Direct relevance

### PART B

**1. Coherence:** Full on the execution side. The paper locates governance failure in architecture rather than in policy quality, and makes the effect boundary the only place governance can be complete.

**2. Alignment:**
- The "three regions" framing puts a name to a gap ABCF also treats as structural: governed capability, ungoverned capability (risk), and governance of non-existent capability ("theater").
- Definition 4.1 is effectively a completeness condition on an execution gate: every expressible effect passes through G, and the governed primitives are expressively complete.
- It is the first mechanized (proof-assistant) artefact located in the execution-gate cluster.

**3. Infringement or tension:**
- The gate governs *effects*, not *authority*. Nothing in the retrieved text says where the policy at G originates, who authorizes it, or how authority is rooted in a named human. Governance is complete over effects and silent on authority origin.
- The work contains no attribution or identifiability layer. Its impossibility result is computability (Rice), which is distinct from both SLT (rank deficiency of the admissible intervention map) and Tibebu (accountability incompleteness). The field now holds three independent impossibility routes to "behavioral governance is structurally insufficient." None of them cites the others on the evidence retrieved.
- The work is industry-authored and the firm's architecture is the proposed remedy. This is a mild promotional context, labelled per §7.

**Known facts:** Author and affiliation as stated. Rice's theorem is the central argument of 2604.27292. Definition 4.1 is as quoted. The proofs are deferred to a companion paper. No citation of Tibebu (2604.07778) or of causal-identifiability literature. The proof repository is public.

**Inferences:** The arXiv IDs place 2604.27292 and 2604.27289 in April 2026. That is before the 4 May 2026 publication of P2 (TR-CNI-1.1) and after the 4 March 2026 publication of P1. Exact dates were not retrieved. *Layer 2 input only:* how this work relates in time and architecture to the execution-gate filing cluster is a question for counsel. It is not a finding.

**Unknowns:** Exact submission dates. Whether the companion papers add authorization semantics at G. Whether "coterminous governance" has any industry or standards uptake. Whether the Rocq/Coq proofs cover the full claims.

**ABCF classification:** Coherent but incomplete

6. **Factual summary:** An interview study of about 25 organisations found that none gave agentic systems unrestricted autonomy. Consequential actions such as payments, data deletion and policy overrides are gated behind human approval. The study also reports "no settled framework for evaluating or assuring agentic AI", unclear responsibility where multiple agents or organisations interact, and human over-reliance on outputs.

9. **Relevance level:** Indirect relevance

### PART B

**1. Coherence:** High. The study describes ABCF-shaped practice from the field: approval gates on consequential action, agent registries, separate agent authentication and audit logs.

**2. Alignment:** The practice aligns with ABCF: bounded autonomy, least privilege, and human approval before irreversible action.

**3. Infringement or tension:** The study reports in plain terms two gaps that ABCF formalizes:
- Over-reliance on outputs means the approval gate exists in name while authority may not be substantive. This is analogical to nominal vs substantive authority.
- Unclear responsibility across interacting agents is analogical to liability-sink topology.
The report states both as practitioner observations. It gives no structural account of either.

**Known facts:** OECD AI Papers series; practitioner interviews; approval-gated action classes and the "no settled framework" finding, as reported by the secondary source.

**Inferences:** The statement that "none reported unrestricted autonomy" describes deployment posture and is not evidence of effective oversight. The same report's over-reliance finding cuts against reading it that way.

**Unknowns:** Series number, authors and exact interview sample (PDF not retrieved). Whether the report recommends any assurance or attribution criterion.

## Item 4 — LG Berlin II, 52 O 62/26 (1 Jun 2026): Google not liable for AI Overviews — divergence from LG München I (backfill)

### PART A

1. **Title:** LG Berlin II, Urteil 52 O 62/26: no trademark use by Google through AI Overviews
2. **Source:** Kanzlei Plutte (practitioner commentary, 16 Jun 2026, updated 22 Jun); the judgment text was not retrieved
3. **Publication date:** Ruling 1 June 2026
4. **Link:** https://www.ra-plutte.de/lg-berlin-keine-markenmaessige-benutzung-durch-ai-overviews/
5. **Jurisdiction / institution:** Germany, Landgericht Berlin II
6. **Factual summary:** The court held Google not liable under trademark and unfair-competition law for AI Overviews that set brand names beside counterfeit "dupes". Its reasoning was that the summaries were not Google's own commercial communication and that Google exercised "no determining influence over the selection and content of sources used by the AI."
7. **Why selected:** This is Layer 1 §8 vector 9. No Frankfurt ruling was located after an eleventh attempt. The search instead surfaced a Berlin ruling that postdates LG München I (26 O 869/26, preliminary injunction, recorded Runs 3–4) and runs the other way. `[PROXIMITY SIGNAL — RJB PUBLICATION: P5]`: attribution by locus of control; admissible observation regime; liability sink.
8. **Relevant ABCF components:** attribution indeterminacy; accountability gaps; causal attribution in AI systems
9. **Relevance level:** Direct relevance

### PART B

**1. Coherence:** Full. Two German regional courts took the same system output and gave opposite answers to "whose statement is this?"

**2. Alignment:** Neither ruling engages causal contribution. Both courts allocate responsibility by proxies:
- **München I:** "independently creates" synthesized content, so the content is Google's own.
- **Berlin II:** "no determining influence" over sources, and a user would read the text as a summary, so the content is not Google's own.
The split is informative. The same generation process supports opposite attribution depending on which observable feature the court treats as decisive.

**3. Infringement or tension:** This is the P5 claim in practice. Courts confined to an admissible observation regime cannot reach the causal question, so they attribute by a chosen observable proxy, and the proxies conflict. Differences in the legal basis (personality rights and defamation in Munich, trademark and UWG in Berlin) partly explain the divergence. The underlying attribution question, whether the system's output is the operator's act, is common to both.

**Known facts:** Court, case number and ruling date as stated. Holding: no trademark use, no UWG liability. Reasoning as quoted from the commentary.

**Inferences:** The divergence will probably reach an appellate court. Google's appeal in the Munich matter was confirmed in June per Runs 3–4. Different claim types may allow the two lines to coexist without direct conflict.

**Unknowns:** The judgment text; whether it is final; the status of the Munich appeal; the Frankfurt ruling, which is still unlocated and may be a misattribution of the Munich decision in earlier commentary.
